INTRODUCTION
[EN] Now let’s apply the week’s ideas to a small business case. Imagine Harbor Box, a startup that prepares locally sourced lunch boxes for marina staff and boat crews.

CASE STUDY
[EN] Harbor Box noticed that crews often had little time to leave the marina for lunch. The founders interviewed staff and learned that predictable delivery time mattered more than a very large menu. They tested a minimum viable product: three lunch options ordered through a simple web page. The target customers were marina teams and boat crews; the buyers were often office or vessel managers, while the end users ate the meals. The value proposition was fresh food delivered to the dock at a reliable time. Harbor Box charged per meal and offered a weekly subscription. It tracked sales volume, food and packaging costs, delivery costs, cash flow, and gross margin. A nearby café was a direct competitor. Grocery stores and customers bringing lunch from home were indirect competition. The startup tested demand with paid pre-orders before renting a kitchen. Its first challenge was reaching break-even without lowering food quality.
