BUSINESS LEARNING — DAY 7: WEEKLY REVIEW
Complete audio lesson narration script

Now let’s apply the week’s ideas to a small business case. Imagine Harbor Box, a startup that prepares locally sourced lunch boxes for marina staff and boat crews.

CASE STUDY
Harbor Box noticed that crews often had little time to leave the marina for lunch. The founders interviewed staff and learned that predictable delivery time mattered more than a very large menu. They tested a minimum viable product: three lunch options ordered through a simple web page. The target customers were marina teams and boat crews; the buyers were often office or vessel managers, while the end users ate the meals. The value proposition was fresh food delivered to the dock at a reliable time. Harbor Box charged per meal and offered a weekly subscription. It tracked sales volume, food and packaging costs, delivery costs, cash flow, and gross margin. A nearby café was a direct competitor. Grocery stores and customers bringing lunch from home were indirect competition. The startup tested demand with paid pre-orders before renting a kitchen. Its first challenge was reaching break-even without lowering food quality.

LISTENING COMPREHENSION
1. What customer pain point is Harbor Box trying to solve?
[Pause for five seconds.]
Answer: Crews have little time to leave the marina and find lunch.
2. Who may be the buyer, and who is the end user?
[Pause for five seconds.]
Answer: A manager may place and pay for the order; marina staff and boat crews are the end users.
3. What is the value proposition?
[Pause for five seconds.]
Answer: Fresh lunch delivered to the dock at a reliable time.
4. Name one direct competitor and one indirect competitor.
[Pause for five seconds.]
Answer: A nearby café is direct competition. Grocery stores or bringing lunch from home are indirect competition.
5. Why did the founders use paid pre-orders?
[Pause for five seconds.]
Answer: They used them to validate demand with real buying behavior before making a larger investment.
6. What does break-even mean for Harbor Box?
[Pause for five seconds.]
Answer: Revenue covers all its costs, so it makes neither a profit nor a loss.
7. How could a weekly subscription affect its business model?
[Pause for five seconds.]
Answer: It could create recurring revenue and make demand easier to plan, if customers continue to renew.
