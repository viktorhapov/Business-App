BUSINESS LEARNING — DAY 1: BUSINESS FUNDAMENTALS
Complete audio lesson narration script

INTRODUCTION
[EN] Welcome to Business Learning. In this lesson, we will build a clear foundation in business through ten practical terms. For each one, you will hear the word, understand the idea, and see how it appears in everyday business. You can listen first and read along whenever that helps. Let’s begin.

TERM 1 — Business
[EN] Now that we have the broad idea of a business, let’s look at the people it serves.
[EN] In simple English: A business solves a problem for customers by offering a product or service. It earns money when customers choose that offer.
[EN] Business examples.
[EN] A family bakery built its business around fresh bread, early opening hours, and friendly service.
[EN] The startup changed its business after interviews showed that customers needed simpler invoicing.
[EN] English in context.
[EN] She runs a small business near the marina.
[EN] Good businesses listen to customers and improve over time.
[EN] Reinforcement.
[EN] A business helps customers solve a real problem.
[EN] A business brings people, products, and work together to serve customers.
[EN] A strong business creates value and can earn enough to continue.
[EN] Quick question.
[EN] What problem does this business solve for its customers?
[PAUSE:5]
[EN] Answer: It solves the problem described in its offer and gives customers a useful way to make progress.

TERM 2 — Customer
[EN] Once we understand the customer, we can look at what a company offers them.
[EN] In simple English: A customer is a person or company that buys a product or service.
[EN] Business examples.
[EN] The restaurant asked its customers which lunch dishes they wanted on the menu.
[EN] A yacht maintenance company sends each customer a clear report after every inspection.
[EN] English in context.
[EN] Our customers value quick delivery and clear updates.
[EN] The company has customers in three countries.
[EN] Reinforcement.
[EN] A customer pays for a solution.
[EN] Every customer has a reason for choosing one business over another.
[EN] When a business understands its customers, it can serve them better.
[EN] Quick question.
[EN] Why might a customer choose one company over another?
[PAUSE:5]
[EN] Answer: A customer may choose it because its offer better fits their needs, budget, timing, or expectations.

TERM 3 — Product
[EN] Some businesses sell products. Others mainly provide useful work, which brings us to service.
[EN] In simple English: A product is something a business makes or sells to meet a customer need.
[EN] Business examples.
[EN] The startup tested its new product with twenty potential users before launch.
[EN] The marine shop added a compact water filter to its product range for weekend sailors.
[EN] English in context.
[EN] The product is easy to set up and use.
[EN] They improved the product after collecting customer feedback.
[EN] Reinforcement.
[EN] A product is an offer customers can choose and use.
[EN] Companies develop products around real customer needs.
[EN] A useful product helps someone get a better result.
[EN] Quick question.
[EN] How can a company learn whether its product meets a real need?
[PAUSE:5]
[EN] Answer: It can speak with potential customers and test a simple version of the product with them.

TERM 4 — Service
[EN] Products and services are different forms of an offer. The next word describes the benefit behind both.
[EN] In simple English: A service is useful work that a business does for a customer.
[EN] Business examples.
[EN] The marina offers a winter storage service for visiting boats.
[EN] A small agency provides social media services to local restaurants.
[EN] English in context.
[EN] The service includes setup, training, and ongoing support.
[EN] Customers expect a helpful service team when something goes wrong.
[EN] Reinforcement.
[EN] A service helps a customer through work or expertise.
[EN] Good service makes the whole customer experience easier.
[EN] A service business delivers a result through actions, not just an object.
[EN] Quick question.
[EN] What is one service a small business could offer?
[PAUSE:5]
[EN] Answer: It could offer delivery, repairs, consulting, cleaning, training, or another useful activity.

TERM 5 — Value
[EN] To understand who may want that benefit, businesses study the market.
[EN] In simple English: Value is the useful benefit a customer gets from a product or service.
[EN] Business examples.
[EN] The booking app creates value by helping guests avoid long phone calls.
[EN] Reliable repairs add value to a yacht by protecting its safety and resale condition.
[EN] English in context.
[EN] Customers pay when they can see the value of an offer.
[EN] The new service offers better value for small teams.
[EN] Reinforcement.
[EN] Value is the benefit behind a purchase.
[EN] A low price is not valuable if the offer does not solve the problem.
[EN] Customers compare the value they receive with the price they pay.
[EN] Quick question.
[EN] What is the difference between price and value?
[PAUSE:5]
[EN] Answer: Price is the money a customer pays; value is the benefit the customer believes they receive.

TERM 6 — Market
[EN] Now let’s follow the money a business receives from those customers.
[EN] In simple English: A market is the group of customers who may want an offer, together with the other offers that compete for them.
[EN] Business examples.
[EN] The company found a market for quiet electric tenders among small yacht owners.
[EN] The café studied the local market before choosing a location near the offices.
[EN] English in context.
[EN] The market is changing as more customers shop online.
[EN] They sell to a niche market of independent hotels.
[EN] Reinforcement.
[EN] A market includes customers and the offers available to them.
[EN] Knowing the market helps a business make better choices.
[EN] A clear target market lets a company focus its time and money.
[EN] Quick question.
[EN] Who are the customers in a business’s target market?
[PAUSE:5]
[EN] Answer: They are the specific people or organizations the business wants and is able to serve.

TERM 7 — Revenue
[EN] That distinction matters because every business must pay costs to operate.
[EN] In simple English: Revenue is all the money a business receives from selling products or services before costs are subtracted.
[EN] Business examples.
[EN] The café increased revenue by opening earlier on busy weekends.
[EN] The online business earns recurring revenue from monthly subscriptions.
[EN] English in context.
[EN] Revenue grew this quarter, but costs grew faster.
[EN] The finance team tracks revenue by product line.
[EN] Reinforcement.
[EN] Revenue is money coming into the business from sales.
[EN] Revenue is counted before operating costs are taken away.
[EN] A business can have high revenue and still lose money.
[EN] Quick question.
[EN] Does revenue show how much money remains after costs?
[PAUSE:5]
[EN] Answer: No. Revenue is counted before costs; the remaining amount is considered when calculating profit.

TERM 8 — Cost
[EN] When we subtract costs from revenue, we can see whether the business earned a profit.
[EN] In simple English: A cost is money a business spends to make, sell, or deliver something.
[EN] Business examples.
[EN] The restaurant reduced food costs by planning portions more carefully.
[EN] Fuel and maintenance are major costs for a marine transport company.
[EN] English in context.
[EN] We need to know the full cost before setting the price.
[EN] Delivery costs rose after the supplier changed its rates.
[EN] Reinforcement.
[EN] Costs are what the business spends to operate.
[EN] Some costs stay similar each month, while others change with sales.
[EN] When costs rise, a company may need to improve efficiency or review prices.
[EN] Quick question.
[EN] Name one cost that a small restaurant has to manage.
[PAUSE:5]
[EN] Answer: It might manage rent, ingredients, staff wages, energy, or delivery costs.

TERM 9 — Profit
[EN] We have covered customers, offers, markets, and money. Finally, let’s connect these ideas into one system.
[EN] In simple English: Profit is the money left after a business subtracts its costs from its revenue.
[EN] Business examples.
[EN] The shop made a small profit after controlling its rent and stock costs.
[EN] The repair company reinvested part of its profit in new equipment.
[EN] English in context.
[EN] The project was popular but did not make a profit.
[EN] Profit gives a business room to invest and prepare for risk.
[EN] Reinforcement.
[EN] Profit remains after costs are paid.
[EN] Revenue is the money from sales; profit is what remains after expenses.
[EN] A sale is useful to a business when it can deliver the offer profitably.
[EN] Quick question.
[EN] What must a business subtract from revenue to calculate profit?
[PAUSE:5]
[EN] Answer: It subtracts its costs and expenses for the period.

TERM 10 — Business Model
[EN] Let’s finish by reviewing the ten ideas you have learned today.
[EN] In simple English: A business model explains how a company creates value for customers and earns money from it.
[EN] Business examples.
[EN] The software company uses a subscription business model and charges customers each month.
[EN] The tour operator earns money by connecting guests with local yacht crews and taking a booking fee.
[EN] English in context.
[EN] They tested a new business model with a small pilot.
[EN] A clear business model shows where revenue comes from and what it costs to deliver the offer.
[EN] Reinforcement.
[EN] A business model connects customer value with revenue and costs.
[EN] It explains how a company serves its customers and earns money.
[EN] The business is the whole operation; the business model explains how that operation works economically.
[EN] Quick question.
[EN] How does this company create value and earn money?
[PAUSE:5]
[EN] Answer: It serves a particular customer need, delivers a useful offer, and receives revenue in a way that can cover its costs.

FINAL REVIEW
[EN] A business solves a customer problem.
[EN] A customer is the person or company buying or using an offer.
[EN] A product is something a business makes or sells.
[EN] A service is useful work done for a customer.
[EN] Value is the benefit the customer receives; price is what they pay.
[EN] A market brings together customers and competing offers.
[EN] Revenue is money from sales before costs are subtracted.
[EN] A cost is money the business spends to operate and deliver its offer.
[EN] Profit is what remains after costs are subtracted from revenue.
[EN] A business model explains how a company creates value and earns money.

CLOSING
[EN] Well done. You have completed Day 1 of Business Learning. You now know ten core terms and how they connect. Choose one business you know and try to describe its customers, offer, value, revenue, costs, and business model in English. See you in the next lesson.
